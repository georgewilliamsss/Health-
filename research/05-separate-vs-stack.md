# Separate vials vs blends vs vendor stacks for the five-peptide course

Date: 2026-10-10

## How this was researched

The sandbox network policy blocked direct page loads, so every fact in this document comes from search-engine page extractions. It is search-verified, not page-verified. Prices are as seen on 2026-10-10 and are list prices that were not confirmed at checkout. Where a figure is an estimate by a panelist, the text says so. The analysis was produced by three panel perspectives (clinical pharmacology and dosing, purity assurance, cost and simplicity) and a judge who synthesised them. Peptide reference protocols come from the project peptide brief. All dosing in this document is a reference range for the GP to set. None of it is a validated clinical dose, and no human trial supports any injectable dose of these peptides.

Regulatory backdrop as of 2026-10-10:

- None of the five peptides (GHK-Cu, TB-500, BPC-157, CJC-1295 no DAC, ipamorelin) is FDA-approved.
- FDA issued warning letters to research-peptide sellers on 31 Mar, 17 Jun and 24 Aug 2026.
- FDA treats bacteriostatic water sold alongside peptides as evidence of intent for human use.
- Peptide Sciences closed on 6 Mar 2026.
- Chameleon Peptides closed to new orders on 27 Sep 2026.

## The decision in one paragraph

Buy all five as separate single-peptide vials, and draw every vial of a given peptide from one lot. Before ordering, require a lot-matched third-party COA for each peptide that shows HPLC purity with the chromatogram, MS identity at the expected mass, net content and endotoxin. All three panelists agree that GHK-Cu, BPC-157 and TB-500 must be singles. Fixed ratios cannot follow the GHK-Cu ramp, the daily BPC-157 dose or the TB-500 load-then-taper. Blend and kit COAs are also the weakest documents in this market: Janoshik reports quantity only for blends, a GHK-Cu-dominated chromatogram can hide problems in the minor components, and one batch COA can be reused across many kits. The one real disagreement is CJC-1295 no DAC plus ipamorelin. Their 1:1 blend matches the 100/100 mcg co-dose, but singles cost only about \$30-60 more per course and add one reconstitution per 28 days. They add no injection, because the two are co-drawn into one syringe anyway. Under the stated rule (purity verifiability wins when the cost or convenience gap is small), singles are the default for these two as well. A 5 mg/5 mg CJC-1295/ipamorelin blend is the only acceptable fallback. It is acceptable only if the chosen vendor lacks well-documented singles and the blend lot COA gives MS identity, quantity and purity for each component from a non-Janoshik lab. If the one-vendor rule cannot supply a documented TB-500 single, first change vendor. Failing that, ask the GP to drop TB-500, which the brief already calls redundant with BPC-157. Do not substitute GLOW, KLOW or a BPC/TB "Wolverine" blend. Buy the full course in one order so each peptide comes from a single lot, and store unopened vials frozen.

Note on bacteriostatic water: the buyer's brief asks for a vendor that sells it on the same site. The panel advises against buying it from the peptide vendor. Source USP bacteriostatic water from a pharmacy or on the GP's prescription instead (details in the quantity template and the GP points).

## Quantity template

### Method

The counts are sized by the 28-day reconstituted window, not by total milligrams. A reconstituted vial is discarded 28 days after first puncture. At low daily doses, the number of 28-day blocks sets the vial count, not the mg total. For example, GHK-Cu at 1 mg/day for 12 weeks needs 84 mg, but that is 3 blocks, so 3 vials of 50 mg (a 50 mg vial would last 50 days but is discarded at day 28).

Reference doses used for the arithmetic (reference ranges only, for the GP to set):

- GHK-Cu: 1 mg/day, possibly 2 mg/day.
- BPC-157: 250-500 mcg/day.
- TB-500: 2-2.5 mg twice weekly for 4-6 weeks, then 2-2.5 mg weekly.
- CJC-1295 no DAC and ipamorelin: 100 mcg of each, once nightly (twice daily gives the higher counts shown).

Water reasoning: bottles are 10 mL because each punctured bottle is discarded at 28 days, so a 30 mL bottle is mostly wasted unless it is used one per block. ### Injection load

- Daily: GHK-Cu, BPC-157, and CJC/ipamorelin co-drawn at bedtime (3 injections).
- TB-500: twice weekly during loading (for example Monday and Thursday), then weekly.
- That is about 23 injections a week during loading. It is about 19 if GHK-Cu and CJC/ipamorelin run 5 days on and 2 off, and 30 or more if BPC-157 or CJC/ipamorelin are twice daily.

### Staging versus one order

The cost panelist advised buying 8 weeks first and topping up after the 4-week labs, since the GP may stop items. The judge favours one full-course order, because a single order locks one lot per peptide and vendors are closing (see the dissent section). If the vendor guarantees a same-lot reorder, or the GP expects to cut items early, staging is reasonable.

## Why GLOW, KLOW and BPC/TB "Wolverine" blends are rejected

### GLOW: reject

GLOW is about 50 mg GHK-Cu + 10 mg BPC-157 + 10 mg TB-500 per 70 mg vial (a 5:1:1 ratio).

- At 1 mg/day GHK-Cu, it gives about 200 mcg/day BPC-157, below the 250-500 mcg range.
- At 1 mg/day GHK-Cu, it gives about 1.4 mg/week TB-500, roughly 30% of the 4-5 mg/week loading dose, and turns TB-500 into a daily drug.
- Reaching TB-500 loading would take about 3.6 mg/day GHK-Cu, which is above the 1-2 mg/day reference range and a copper-load concern. It would also push BPC-157 to about 714 mcg/day, above the 500 mcg ceiling.
- A hold or taper of GHK-Cu (a 30-on/30-off cycle, or a copper rise) stops all three peptides at once.
- All three start on the same day, so side effects cannot be attributed to one agent.
- GHK-Cu is about 71% of the mass and dominates the chromatogram, so the 10 mg components are the least documented.
- With 70 mg + 3.5 mL, 1 mg GHK-Cu is 7 units.
- At 1 mg/day only 28 of 50 mg GHK-Cu is used before the day-28 discard.
- The brief already lists GLOW under products to avoid.
- It saves money (about \$150-250 over 8 weeks) and cuts injections to about 14 a week, but it delivers only 11-22 mg of TB-500 against the 28-30 mg reference and still needs a TB-500 single on top.

### KLOW: reject

KLOW is GLOW plus 10 mg KPV. It has all the GLOW problems and adds KPV, which the GP did not request.

### BPC-157 + TB-500 1:1 ("Wolverine"): reject as a default

- At a flat 500/500 mcg/day the weekly totals roughly match (28 mg each over 8 weeks, 42 mg over 12), but there is no loading or maintenance shape.
- TB-500 can be neither loaded and then tapered nor dropped on its own.
- At 250 mcg/day BPC-157, a blend dosed to the TB-500 8-week total gives 2x the target BPC-157 (28 mg against 14 mg). Dosed to BPC-157, it gives only half the TB-500.
- At 250-500 mcg/day BPC-157, TB-500 lands at 1.75-3.5 mg/week, short of loading. Hitting 5 mg/week needs about 700 mcg/day BPC-157, above range.
- Label overfill (Protide TitrateLab: 137 of 195 doses over target; Verified Peptides measured 8-13% over label) shifts both components together, and quantity-only blend tests may not show it per component.
- The brief separately lists long-term BPC-157 plus TB-500 co-use as one to avoid.
- Blend COAs are often Janoshik quantity-only.
- It is a last-resort fallback only: the GP must explicitly accept flat daily dosing with no TB-500 taper, and the lot COA must give per-component MS identity and quantity plus a full panel from a non-Janoshik lab. Changing vendor or dropping TB-500 is better.

### When a 1:1 CJC/ipamorelin blend is acceptable

It is the only acceptable blend, as a conditional fallback, not the default.

- The ratio matches the 100/100 mcg co-dose. The side effects are shared class effects of the GH axis anyway.
- Use it only if the vendor lacks documented singles AND the blend lot COA gives all of: MS identity for each component, quantity for each component, HPLC purity from a non-Janoshik lab, and endotoxin.
- A blend whose only data is Janoshik quantity means: buy singles or change vendor.
- The GP must write doses as mcg of each component, not as units of blend.
- Limits: unequal dosing (for example ipamorelin 200 mcg against CJC 100 mcg) cannot be followed, and the GP cannot stop one component alone if a side effect appears (flushing or head rush from the GHRH side; hunger or water retention from the ghrelin side).
- Some sources are ambiguous on whether "300 mcg" is each peptide or the combined dose, which is another reason to write per-component doses.
- Evidence seen: Kylo lists two-lab HPLC results for its CJC/ipamorelin page (Freedom Diagnostics 99.62%, Vanguard 98.56%). This is the best blend evidence seen, but it is not confirmed to be per-component.
- If a vendor's blend lot COA is genuinely component-resolved and its singles are poorly documented, the blend is the better-verified purchase and should win.

### Vendor kits and stacks: avoid

Examples are "Recovery & Repair" bundles and a "CJC+IPA stack". Avoid them unless every vial carries its own printed lot and a matching COA.

- Fixed counts do not match the 28-day plan. One 10 mg TB-500 vial is only about 2 weeks of loading.
- They may reuse one batch COA across many kits.
- Bundled bacteriostatic water is the FDA human-use flag.
- They offer no pharmacological advantage.
- A kit is acceptable only as a price wrapper around individually lot-labelled vials, each with its own COA, with counts matched to the 28-day plan. Never accept a single kit-level COA.

## The purity-verification argument

The deciding criterion is how much public, per-batch purity information exists for what the vendor ships. Singles beat blends and kits on that criterion.

Why singles are easier to verify:

- A single-peptide COA gives one main HPLC peak, one purity percentage and one MS identity.
- Finnrick and TitrateLab results are organised per peptide, with batch numbers, so a single vial maps one-to-one onto independent testing.
- A single COA can show net content, so the GP can correct the reconstitution arithmetic for overfill.
- The identity traps are checkable only in a single-lot MS result: TB-500 (Ac-LKKTETQ, about 889 Da, versus full-length thymosin beta-4, about 4,960 Da) and CJC-1295 (no DAC about 3,368 Da versus DAC about 3,647 Da). Molecular masses are approximate, so check them against the theoretical mass stated on the COA.

Why blend and kit COAs are weaker:

- Blend COAs are quantity-only. Janoshik reports on blends give quantity, not purity, so each component's purity is not established.
- A multi-component chromatogram makes a single purity figure ambiguous. Treat any single "purity" number on a blend as uninformative.
- In GLOW, GHK-Cu is about 71% of the mass, so identity or impurity problems in the BPC-157 and TB-500 can hide behind it.
- Independent testers rarely test blend lots.
- Reused COAs: a vendor can reuse one batch COA across many kit or blend lots, which weakens per-vial lot traceability.
- Overfill is invisible per component unless the COA quantifies each one.
- Blend evidence that was seen and judged acceptable: Chameleon BPC/TB Batch B003 (Janoshik identity and amount plus a Vanguard full panel); Protide BPC/TB COA #2603230047 (Freedom Diagnostics, identity and endotoxin pass); Kylo CJC/ipamorelin (Freedom 99.62%, Vanguard 98.56%). For any blend, require component-resolved identity and content plus a purity figure from a non-Janoshik lab.

What a lot must show before it counts as verified:

1. The lot number printed on the vial matches the COA lot number. Reject a vial with no lot, or with a lot the vendor's COA ledger does not list.
2. Testing by a named third-party lab, with the report checkable on the lab's own site where possible (Janoshik verification keys, Freedom Diagnostics links). The COA must not predate the lot.
3. HPLC purity of 98% or more for a single peptide, with the chromatogram, not just a number.
4. MS identity matching the expected mass.
5. Quantity per vial against the label.
6. Endotoxin (LAL / USP <85>) on that lot. This is often missing: TitrateLab shows endotoxin on only 30 of 278 Protide certificates, so ask for it.
7. Sterility (USP <71>) is rarely tested. Treat its absence as a known residual risk, and prefer a vendor that does test it.

Workflow:

- Buy every vial of a peptide in one order and confirm before shipping that all come from one lot. That is 5 lot checks, with no extra checks for water bought at a pharmacy.
- Save PDFs or screenshots of every COA at purchase. Vendor sites and COA pages disappear when a vendor closes.
- Cross-check each peptide on Finnrick or TitrateLab for the chosen vendor, judged peptide by peptide and not by the vendor's overall grade.
- Transparency is not quality. Verified Peptides covers all five as singles and publishes Finnrick results, but its pass rates (BPC-157 44%, CJC-1295 31%, ipamorelin 58%; 18 of 36 failed) disqualify it for those three. No independent tester has GHK-Cu or TB-500 data for it.
- For the highest identity risks (TB-500 and CJC-1295 no DAC), consider buying one extra vial of the same lot for independent HPLC+MS.
- Single-lab dependence (for example Protide, all Freedom Diagnostics) is acceptable only if endotoxin is shown for the specific lots bought.

Catalog constraint: no shortlisted vendor was confirmed to sell all five as well-documented singles.

- Kylo: GHK-Cu and BPC-157 singles plus the CJC/ipamorelin blend, and no TB-500 single seen.
- Protide: BPC-157 and TB-500 singles, GHK-Cu per the brief, and no CJC-1295 or ipamorelin seen.
- Core: GHK-Cu, GLOW and the CJC/ipamorelin blend, and no TB-500 single seen.
- Panda: BPC-157 and TB-500 singles, CJC-1295 and ipamorelin per Trustpilot only, and no GHK-Cu single or bacteriostatic water seen.
- Verified Peptides: all five as singles, but poor independent results as above.
- Chameleon: closed to new orders since 27 Sep 2026.

Vendor conditions that follow from this (summary of the judge's list):

1. Coverage: singles for all five, each with a lot-matched third-party COA meeting the checks above.
2. Substitution order if singles cannot all be met: (a) the CJC/ipamorelin blend under the conditions above; (b) for a missing TB-500 single, change vendor or have the GP drop TB-500; (c) a BPC/TB blend only under the strict conditions above; (d) never GLOW or KLOW.
3. Independent data: prefer public Finnrick or TitrateLab results for these specific peptides. Full catalog coverage does not outweigh poor independent results.
4. Exclusions: no vendor named in the 31 Mar, 17 Jun or 24 Aug 2026 FDA warning letters; no Chameleon; no listings priced far below market (for example BPC-157 under about \$3/mg).
5. Lots: confirm one lot per peptide before shipping, and check printed vial lots against COAs on delivery.
6. Water: do not buy the vendor's bacteriostatic water or diluent.
7. Shipping: fast shipping to Ohio (432xx) is a tiebreaker only. No Ohio-specific factor was found in the inputs.

## Dissent

CJC/ipamorelin form (clinical and cost perspectives against purity):

- Two of three panelists recommended the 1:1 CJC/ipamorelin blend outright. It halves the vial count and reconstitutions and reproduces the protocol exactly.
- This is kept as the named fallback. If a vendor's blend lot COA is component-resolved and its singles are poorly documented, the blend wins.

Purchase staging (cost perspective):

- The cost panelist advised buying 8 weeks first and topping up after the 4-week labs.
- The judgment favours one full-course order to lock a single lot per peptide, given 2026 vendor closures and enforcement. Store unopened lyophilised vials at -20 degrees C.
- If the vendor can guarantee a same-lot reorder, or the GP expects to cut items early, staging is reasonable.

BPC/TB blend ranking (clinical perspective):

- The clinical panelist ranked a BPC/TB 1:1 blend as an acceptable second substitution if the GP accepts fixed daily dosing.
- The judgment ranks it below changing vendor or dropping TB-500, because its COAs are usually quantity-only.

Quantity differences that remain within a defensible range:

- TB-500 at 12 weeks: 4 vials (clinical, 4-week load) against 5 (brief, purity and cost, 6-week load).
- BPC-157 at 500 mcg/day: 4 against 5 vials.
- Bacteriostatic water: 6 x 10 mL against 3 x 30 mL.
- Syringes: the purity panelist's count assumed 5-6 separate injections a day, ignoring the usual CJC/ipamorelin co-draw.

Injection burden (cost perspective):

- About 23 injections a week during loading is a real adherence burden. GLOW would cut it to about 14.
- This was rejected on dosing and verifiability, but the burden is real.

## Points to discuss with the GP

All doses below are reference ranges for the GP to set.

1. Whether all five are needed. The biggest cuts in cost, injections and risk come from changing the stack, not the vial format. TB-500 is redundant with BPC-157 for a healthy adult, the brief lists long-term combined use as one to avoid, and dropping it also solves the vendor-coverage gap. The CJC/ipamorelin pair has the weakest general-health case.
2. Lawful pharmacy-grade alternatives with real quality control: topical GHK-Cu (the only route with human RCT data) in place of injectable, and 503A sermorelin or tesamorelin in place of CJC-1295/ipamorelin.
3. Regulatory and quality status. None of the five is FDA-approved, all are WADA-prohibited, and research-use-only vials are not made under GMP and are rarely sterility-tested. Finnrick data show about 42% of tested vials failing on dose, purity or identity, and about 15% of endotoxin-tested vials positive. Bring the actual lot COAs to the GP and agree in writing who owns the dosing.
4. Baseline labs: IGF-1, fasting glucose/HbA1c, blood pressure, serum copper and ceruloplasmin, plus age-appropriate cancer screening. Repeat IGF-1, glucose and copper at about 4 weeks, and decide on weeks 9-12 at that review.
5. Hard stops: any cancer history or active malignancy (four of the five are pro-angiogenic or raise IGF-1), pregnancy or breastfeeding, Wilson's disease or a copper disorder, diabetes or prediabetes without monitoring, heart failure or oedema, pituitary disease, and drug-tested sport or military status.
6. Stop rules: IGF-1 above the age-normal range, rising glucose, oedema or carpal-tunnel symptoms, raised copper, injection-site infection, or brown or cloudy GHK-Cu solution.
7. Staggered starts, one new agent every 5-7 days (for example BPC-157 250 mcg, then TB-500 loading, then GHK-Cu 1 mg, then CJC/ipamorelin 100/100 mcg nightly), so any reaction can be traced to one agent.
8. Ask the GP to write each dose in mg or mcg AND in syringe units at the stated reconstitution volume. Correct for the lot's measured net content on the COA, since overfill of 8-13% or more is common.
9. Schedule choices that change the vial counts: daily versus 5-on/2-off for GHK-Cu and CJC/ipamorelin, once versus twice nightly for CJC/ipamorelin, the length of TB-500 loading, and 8 versus 12 weeks.
10. Which syringe-mixing is allowed. CJC-1295 and ipamorelin may be co-drawn by convention. Do not combine BPC-157, TB-500 or GHK-Cu without GP agreement, since there are no compatibility data. Keep the GHK-Cu injection site separate.
11. A prescription or pharmacy route for USP bacteriostatic water and insulin syringes, plus storage advice: unopened vials frozen, reconstituted vials at 2-8 degrees C and protected from light, discarded after 28 days.
