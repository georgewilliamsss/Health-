# US Regulatory and Legal Status of the Five Peptides

**Date:** 2026-10-10

**Subject:** GHK-Cu, TB-500, BPC-157, CJC-1295 (no DAC) and Ipamorelin, suggested by a local GP in Columbus, Ohio.

**Purpose:** Background research behind a one-page purchasing decision. This is a summary of what the research found. It is not legal advice and not medical advice.

> **How this was researched.** The analysts could not open web pages from the research environment (sandbox network policy). Every citation in this document is search-verified, not page-verified: the facts come from search-tool extractions of the cited URLs. Two research passes were run. The later pass (which checked the 2026 developments) corrects the earlier one where they differ, and the differences are flagged in the text and listed near the end. Items marked "secondary" come from law-firm blogs, trade press, vendor-run sites or community posts rather than from FDA or court records. Items marked "unverified" could not be confirmed at all. Check fda.gov directly before acting on any status below, because it is changing quickly.

## Bottom line for the buyer

1. **None of the five is an approved drug, and none is a controlled substance.** No FDA-approved product contains any of them. None is scheduled under the Controlled Substances Act, and Ohio's drug-possession statute (ORC 2925.11) covers controlled substances only.
2. **Only one lawful prescription route exists today: non-injectable (topical) GHK-Cu.** An Ohio GP can prescribe it through a licensed 503A compounding pharmacy. Injectable GHK-Cu, BPC-157, TB-500, CJC-1295 and ipamorelin have no lawful compounding route as of 10 Oct 2026.
3. **BPC-157 and TB-500 could change, but have not yet.** FDA's advisory committee recommended both for the 503A bulks list on 23 July 2026 (8-6 with one abstention, against FDA staff advice). That vote is advisory. Final rulemaking is not expected before 2027. On 7 Oct 2026 the Washington Post reported that HHS is drafting a temporary enforcement-discretion stopgap. It has not been issued, and which peptides it would cover is unknown.
4. **CJC-1295 (no DAC) and ipamorelin are not part of the 2026 loosening.** The committee voted against both in late 2024, and neither was on the 2026 agenda. Telehealth clinics that advertise them operate outside 503A eligibility. That is a legal and quality red flag.
5. **Buying "research use only" (RUO) product for your own use is a grey area.** Possession is not a federal drug crime, and no prosecution of an individual buyer was found. FDA and DOJ action targets sellers. A buyer has no consumer-protection recourse, because the product is sold as "not for human use". Whether any Ohio statute reaches an individual's possession of a non-controlled prescription-only drug was not verified.
6. **FDA enforcement against RUO sellers is active and comes in waves** (31 Mar, 17 Jun and 24 Aug 2026). FDA treats bacteriostatic water, reconstitution kits and dosing calculators sold with peptides as evidence of human-use intent, and "research use only" labelling does not protect a seller. Vendors can vanish or lose payment processing after an order is paid, so favour small orders over stockpiling.
7. **Quality is the biggest practical problem with the grey market.** An April 2026 preprint (not peer-reviewed) found that 41.6% to 71.1% of grey-market samples failed quality criteria and 15% had measurable endotoxin, with no link between endotoxin and HPLC purity. A prescription from a 503A pharmacy gives much better assurance, where it is lawful.
8. **Ohio and sport.** The Ohio Board of Pharmacy actively disciplines clinics and distributors that hold BPC-157 and ipamorelin. A GP suggesting these peptides does not make an RUO purchase lawful or supervised. BPC-157, TB-500, CJC-1295 and ipamorelin are prohibited at all times under WADA rules, which matters only if you compete in tested sport.

### Terms used in this document

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| FDCA | The Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act. Introducing an unapproved new drug into interstate commerce (21 USC 331(d) and 355(a)) and misbranding are prohibited acts that attach to sellers and distributors. |
| 503A pharmacy | A state-licensed pharmacy that compounds a drug for an individual patient on a valid prescription. |
| 503A bulks list | The formal list of bulk drug substances a 503A pharmacy may compound with. Adding a substance needs FDA rulemaking. |
| Category 1 | FDA's interim list of substances for which it does not intend to act against a 503A pharmacy that compounds them on a valid prescription, if the other 503A conditions are met. |
| Category 2 | FDA's interim list of substances that may present significant safety risks. Pharmacies are not covered by the interim enforcement-discretion policy. |
| PCAC | The Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee. Its votes are advice to FDA, not decisions. |
| RUO | "Research use only", the label under which most grey-market peptides are sold. |
| COA | Certificate of analysis, the lab report a vendor publishes for a batch. |
| BAC water | Bacteriostatic water, used to reconstitute freeze-dried peptide for injection. |
| WADA | World Anti-Doping Agency. Its list applies to athletes in tested sport. |

### Background: 2023 to 2024

- In 2023 FDA placed BPC-157, TB-500, CJC-1295 and ipamorelin in Category 2 (significant safety risks). Ipamorelin was added on 29 Sep 2023. Injectable GHK-Cu was also in Category 2 (the sources do not give the date).
- FDA's Category 2 page cites immunogenicity risk for BPC-157 and for injectable GHK-Cu, and serious adverse events for CJC-1295 (increased heart rate and a systemic vasodilatory reaction).
- In September 2024 the nominators withdrew the nominations for CJC-1295 and ipamorelin. The CJC-1295 nominators had given inconsistent information about which CJC-1295 substance they meant (FDA briefing document, per search extract).
- On 29 Oct 2024 PCAC voted against adding ipamorelin (with L-theanine, ibutamoren and kisspeptin-10). On 4 Dec 2024 it voted against CJC-1295, reported as unanimous, citing a lack of evidence of effectiveness. Tallies are secondary.
- Sources: https://a4pc.org/news/pcac-votes-against-four-nominated-bulk-drug-substances, https://www.fda.gov/media/183819/download, https://www.fda.gov/media/185412/download, https://www.fda.gov/media/185642/download, https://www.fda.gov/drugs/human-drug-compounding/certain-bulk-drug-substances-use-compounding-may-present-significant-safety-risks

### April 2026: twelve peptides leave Category 2

- **What happened.** HHS Secretary Kennedy announced the change on 15 Apr 2026. FDA updated its category lists on 22 Apr 2026 (one secondary source says 23 Apr).
- **The twelve peptides.** BPC-157, TB-500, injectable GHK-Cu, KPV, MOTS-c, Semax, Epitalon, Emideltide (DSIP), LL-37 (cathelicidin), Dihexa acetate, PEG-MGF and Melanotan II.
- **Why.** The nominators withdrew their nominations. FDA said this was not a new safety finding.
- **Correction on the mechanism.** The earlier pass cited a 16 Apr 2026 Federal Register notice as the removal. The later pass found no Federal Register notice that made the removal. It was an update to FDA's web category lists. The Federal Register document dated 16 Apr 2026 (FR Doc 2026-07361) is the notice of the July PCAC meeting, and it opened a public comment docket.
- **Legal effect.** Leaving Category 2 does not make a substance compoundable. The twelve are not in Category 1, so FDA's interim enforcement-discretion policy does not cover them (Frier Levitt and Polsinelli, both secondary).
- **Status.** Confirmed in substance; mechanism corrected.
- **Sources.** https://www.frierlevitt.com/articles/fda-peptides-do-not-compound-list-update-2026/, https://www.polsinelli.com/publications/what-fdas-latest-actions-mean-for-peptide-compounding, https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=2e55b76a-3173-4e04-beda-bf021202f18d, https://www.spakinect.com/news/fda-removes-12-peptides-503a-category-2-compounding, https://ssrpinstitute.org/news/fda-announces-change-in-status-of-12-peptides/, https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/04/16/2026-07361/pharmacy-compounding-advisory-committee-notice-of-meeting-establishment-of-a-public-docket-request

### May 2026: the GHK-Cu list correction

- On 22 Apr 2026 non-injectable GHK-Cu was also taken off Category 1.
- On 5 May 2026 a nominator clarified that it had meant to withdraw only the injectable route. FDA then put non-injectable GHK-Cu back in Category 1. - FDA says it intends to consult PCAC on GHK-Cu before the end of February 2027. Per the earlier pass, LL-37, Melanotan II, Dihexa and PEG-MGF are in the same consult.

### July 2026: the PCAC meeting and votes

- PCAC met on 23 and 24 Jul 2026. The committee voted against FDA staff and recommended **six of seven** peptides for the 503A bulks list: BPC-157, TB-500, KPV, MOTS-c, Semax and Epitalon. It rejected Emideltide (DSIP).
- **Votes.** News reports say BPC-157, TB-500 and KPV each passed 8-6 with one abstention on 23 Jul. Reports of the MOTS-c vote differ (7-5 with two abstentions, or 8-6). These tallies come from news coverage (NCPA, AJMC, ABC News, Medical Daily, STAT, McDermott), not from FDA's official meeting record.
- **FDA staff view.** FDA's briefing documents recommended against listing BPC-157 (free base and acetate). The reasons given were no USP monograph, missing data on impurities, aggregates and endotoxin, and a possible immune response. The use under review was ulcerative colitis. The earlier pass reports the same staff position for TB-500, and says staff opposed all seven.
- **What it means.** The vote is advisory. FDA must decide and then go through notice-and-comment rulemaking before any substance is added to the bulks list. The earlier pass estimates 6 to 12 months or more, and "no earlier than 2027". As of 10 Oct 2026 no FDA decision or proposed rule was found. A docket number, FDA-2025-N-6895, was reported but is unverified.
- **Not on the agenda:** CJC-1295, ipamorelin and GHK-Cu.
- **Status.** Confirmed.

## FDA enforcement against research-peptide sellers

### Legal basis

- The FD&C Act's prohibited acts attach to sellers and distributors, not to people who buy for themselves. FDA infers "intended use" from the seller's marketing.
- A "research use only" label does not protect a seller whose site, FAQs, dosing content or social media imply human use.
- Criminal enforcement has also reached RUO sellers (see the table of other actions below).
- No FTC action against an RUO peptide seller was found. Law firms note that FTC and state attorney-general deceptive-practice exposure also exists for sellers.
- No federal prosecution of an individual buying domestic RUO peptides for personal use was found.

### What the letters say about bacteriostatic water and "research use only"

- **"Research use only" does not override intent.** FDA looks at the whole marketing picture. The Gram Peptides letter says the RUO labelling does not override evidence of human-use intent. The Royal Peptides and Peptide Partners letters rejected the RUO labelling, and so did the Feb 2025 USApeptide.com letter.
- **BAC water is treated as a drug.** FDA treats bacteriostatic water sold by RUO vendors alongside peptides as an unapproved drug intended for injection. It appears in the Gram, Prime Sciences, PekCura, Mile High, Pink Pony, Peak Performance, Royal, NuScience and Tex Peptides letters, and in Peptide Partners' items.
- **Kits, calculators and dosing content are evidence of intent.** The letters cite a reconstitution kit with BAC water and a syringe (Prime Sciences), a peptide calculator (Tex Peptides) and social-media dosing "cheat sheets" (Mile High).
- **Some products are named as not compoundable.** The Prime Sciences letter says retatrutide is not eligible for 503A compounding.
- **Practical reading.** A vendor that bundles BAC water, kits or calculators is a higher enforcement target. Whether FDA-approved BAC water is available on prescription was not verified.

## The prescription and compounding route

This section reflects the later pass where it differs from the earlier one. It is a federal picture as of 10 Oct 2026. Ohio Board of Pharmacy rules on compounding were not checked (unverified). A 503A pharmacy in Ohio is bound by the same federal bulks policy.

### Status by peptide

| Peptide | Lawful 503A route today? | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| GHK-Cu, topical or other non-injectable | **Yes, conditionally** | Back in Category 1 after the May 2026 correction. FDA does not intend to act against a 503A pharmacy that compounds it on a valid prescription, if the other 503A conditions are met (for example, API from an FDA-registered source with a COA). An Ohio GP can prescribe it through a licensed 503A pharmacy. Topical cosmetic GHK-Cu serums are also lawful over the counter. FDA plans a PCAC review before Feb 2027. |
| GHK-Cu, injectable | **No** | The nomination was withdrawn. It is neither in Category 1 nor on the bulks list, and the interim policy does not cover it. FDA has flagged immunogenicity, aggregation and impurity concerns. No route is expected soon, because no injectable nomination is pending. |
| BPC-157 | **No** | It left Category 2 in April 2026, but that does not authorise compounding. PCAC recommended it 8-6 in July. Next steps are FDA rulemaking (no date) or the reported HHS stopgap (not issued, scope unknown). If a stopgap is issued and covers BPC-157, a GP could prescribe it through a 503A pharmacy. |
| TB-500 | **No** | Same position as BPC-157. |
| CJC-1295, no DAC | **No** | FDA lists it as nominated but withdrawn, citing increased heart rate, a systemic vasodilatory reaction and limited clinical data. An FDA briefing found the criteria weigh against listing it. It was not part of the July 2026 review. No change is expected. |
| Ipamorelin | **No** | The later check found it still in active Category 2 (added 29 Sep 2023), with FDA citing immunogenicity concerns and serious adverse events, including a death, after intravenous use. It was not part of the 2026 review. No change is expected. |

### Points to keep in mind

- **A stopgap would not fix supply at once.** If HHS issues an enforcement-discretion stopgap, the Alliance for Pharmacy Compounding expects it to carry conditions such as patient disclosure and adverse-event reporting. The earlier pass estimated that prescription BPC-157 and TB-500 could then be available through Ohio-licensed pharmacies within weeks to months. That is a projection, not a report.
- **API supply may be the limit.** Frier Levitt notes that pharmaceutical-grade API for many of these peptides exists only in research grade, and research-grade material cannot lawfully be used in compounding. A lawful 503A pharmacy must use bulk API from an FDA-registered establishment with a COA.

### Telehealth clinic offers that are not 503A-eligible

Clinics advertise CJC-1295, ipamorelin and injectable GHK-Cu even though none is 503A-eligible. The prices below come from vendor-written secondary sources (meto.co, rxfox.co, mypeptidematch.com, healingmaps.com). Ohio-specific prices were not found.

|---|---|
| CJC-1295 with ipamorelin, medication only | About \$200 to \$400 per month |
| CJC-1295 with ipamorelin, all-inclusive | About \$300 to \$600 per month |
| Initial consult / follow-up consult | \$200 to \$500 / \$100 to \$300 |

A pharmacy supplying CJC-1295, ipamorelin or injectable GHK-Cu is outside 503A eligibility. That is itself a quality and legal red flag.

### Other points

- **FDA-approved GH-axis alternatives.** Tesamorelin (Egrifta, approved for HIV lipodystrophy) and sermorelin (formerly approved as Geref) would be lawful prescription routes. Their current compounding status and cost were not verified. Note that tesamorelin was cited in two August 2026 warning letters as a product RUO vendors sold, so buying it from an RUO vendor is not a lawful route.
- **BAC water.** The 2026 letters treat BAC water sold by peptide vendors as an unapproved drug. Whether FDA-approved BAC water can be had on prescription was not verified.
- **Monitoring.** The earlier pass advises involving a prescriber for monitoring, for example IGF-1 levels for the GH secretagogues.

### Purity: compounding route versus research-grade

| Factor | 503A pharmacy (where lawful) | RUO vendor |
|---|---|---|
| API source | Bulk API from an FDA-registered establishment with a COA | No obligation; the vendor chooses the lab |
| Sterility and endotoxin | Subject to state board inspection and USP sterile-compounding standards for injectables (USP 797; general knowledge, not re-verified) | No sterility, endotoxin or GMP obligation |
| Prescriber and pharmacist oversight | Yes | No |
| Adverse-event and recall pathway | Yes | No recall mechanism |
| Recourse | Professional-liability recourse | Effectively none ("not for human use") |

An April 2026 preprint (Mendias and Awan, preprints.org, not peer-reviewed) analysed a public grey-market testing dataset. It found 41.6% to 71.1% of samples failed the applied quality criteria and 15% had measurable endotoxin. Endotoxin showed no relationship to HPLC purity (R squared below 0.01). A high-purity HPLC COA therefore says nothing about sterility or endotoxin.

**Questions to put to a prescriber or pharmacy** (derived from the points above, not from a source checklist):

1. Which FDA category is the substance in today, and is it Category 1?
2. Is the pharmacy licensed by the Ohio Board of Pharmacy, and where does it source its API?
3. Is a COA from an FDA-registered API source available?
4. Which monitoring (for example IGF-1) will the prescriber arrange?

## Ohio-specific notes

1. **Possession.** ORC 2925.11 criminalises possession of controlled substances only. These peptides are not scheduled, so it does not apply. No reported Ohio prosecution of an individual buyer was found.
2. **Selling and distributing.** ORC 4729.51 restricts who may possess for sale, sell, distribute or deliver "dangerous drugs" (prescription-only drugs as defined in ORC 4729.01(F)). It is a commercial and wholesale provision, not a personal-possession offence.
3. **Unverified.** Whether any Ohio statute makes an individual's possession of a non-controlled prescription-only "dangerous drug" without a prescription an offence was not verified.
4. **The Ohio Board of Pharmacy is the most visible state enforcer on peptides** (per Frier Levitt). It has issued and published summary suspension orders and settlements against clinics, med spas and terminal distributors of dangerous drugs where BPC-157, ipamorelin and AOD-9604 were found. Several cases did not involve pharmacies at all.
5. **A published Board order.** One order shows a summary suspension dated 3 Feb 2026, with the order effective 16 Apr 2026, itemising BPC-157 vials. The indexed text was fragmentary.
6. **Board guidance.** "Ten Common Prescriber Clinic and Medical Spa Violations" (updated 8 Dec 2025) is the Board's guidance for clinics. A secondary source (peptidelaws.com) says a February 2026 Board guidance warns prescribers against sourcing peptides marketed as "research chemicals" or "not for human consumption". That claim is unverified. Law-firm summaries (BMD) say Ohio treats peptides for human use as prescription drugs and tell clinics to avoid RUO-labelled product.
7. **What this means for the GP.** An Ohio-licensed physician who prescribes or administers RUO peptides, or who sources injectable BPC-157, TB-500, CJC-1295 or ipamorelin, risks Board and Medical Board action. A GP's suggestion does not make an RUO purchase lawful or supervised. A lawful prescription route today covers only non-injectable GHK-Cu.
8. **Telehealth.** An out-of-state telehealth prescriber must be licensed in Ohio to prescribe to an Ohio resident, and a dispensing pharmacy must hold an Ohio Board licence or non-resident registration. Both are general rules and were not verified in this research.
9. **Customs.** Customs is not relevant for a domestic vendor. Several vendors on the test lists are based in China or Hong Kong. For them, customs delays and the FDA import alerts that some secondary sources say remain in force for TB-500 and BPC-157 could matter (unverified).
10. **Local availability.** No Ohio shipping information was found for any vendor, and no Ohio-specific vendor or Ohio compounding price was found.
11. **Statutes.** ORC 4729.01(F) (authenticated version dated 9 Jun 2026), ORC 4729.51 and ORC 2925.11.

## Practical risks of research-grade peptides

1. **Identity and dose.** Vials may be mislabelled, under- or over-dosed, or contain a different peptide. One vendor-hosted secondary report puts about 30% of samples as mislabelled, mis-quantified or contaminated (unverified). The Mendias and Awan preprint found 41.6% to 71.1% failing quality frameworks. A "US vendor" is not necessarily US-manufactured: the Paradigm Peptides case involved product sourced from China and India and sold as US-made.
2. **Sterility and endotoxin.** About 15% of samples in the preprint dataset had measurable endotoxin, uncorrelated with purity. Reconstituting freeze-dried peptide at home with bacteriostatic water carries infection and pyrogen risk. A per-batch HPLC or mass-spec COA rarely includes sterility (USP 71) or endotoxin (LAL, USP 85) testing. The earlier pass advises preferring vendors that publish all three per lot and verifying COAs directly with the testing lab.
3. **COA reliability.** Paradigm Peptides' forged certificates show a vendor-published COA is weak evidence on its own. A June 2026 community comment says one batch's COA can be reused across kits, and a common complaint is a wrong or empty vial. Janoshik tests on blends measure quantity, not purity (per a lab-community summary).
4. **Immunogenicity and aggregation.** FDA cited immunogenicity risk for BPC-157 and injectable GHK-Cu, and adverse events (tachycardia, vasodilatory reactions) for CJC-1295. For ipamorelin it cites immunogenicity concerns and serious adverse events, including a death after intravenous use.
5. **Thin evidence.** Human efficacy data for BPC-157 and TB-500 are minimal (STAT, Feb 2026). FDA staff recommended against BPC-157 in July 2026 (and, per the earlier pass, against the other peptides on that agenda). ECRI and ISMP warned that safety-data gaps remain.
6. **No recourse.** The product is sold "not for human use", so there is effectively no product-liability or consumer-protection recourse, no recall notice and no pharmacist oversight. Vendors can vanish after FDA or DOJ action, as the 24 Aug 2026 recipients may, and several have closed or been reported as exit scams (see "Vendors to avoid").
7. **BAC water and kits.** Vendors who sell BAC water or reconstitution kits are higher enforcement targets, and their stock can disappear mid-course.
8. **Regulatory flux.** The status may change within weeks (an HHS stopgap) or months (2027 rulemaking). Re-check FDA's 503A categories page before purchase.
9. **Sport.** BPC-157, TB-500, CJC-1295 and ipamorelin are prohibited at all times under WADA. No therapeutic use exemptions are available for GH secretagogues.
10. **Medical oversight.** A GP recommending RUO peptides without a prescription and pharmacy supply is outside standard practice in Ohio (earlier pass). Involve a prescriber for monitoring.
