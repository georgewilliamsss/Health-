# US Vendor Investigation: Where to Buy the Five Peptides and Bacteriostatic Water

**Date:** 2026-10-10

**Subject:** GHK-Cu, TB-500, BPC-157, CJC-1295 (no DAC) and Ipamorelin, plus bacteriostatic water, for an 8-12 week course suggested by a GP, bought by an adult in Columbus, Ohio (ZIP 432xx) from one US research-peptide vendor.

**Purpose:** Background research behind a one-page purchasing decision. It is a record of what the research found and how sure we are. It is not legal advice and not medical advice.

> **How this was researched.** The sandbox network policy blocked direct page loads. Every fact below comes from search-engine page extractions, so it is search-verified, not page-verified. No vendor page, lab PDF or lab portal was opened by the researchers, and no order, checkout or login was tested. Prices, stock, COA (certificate of analysis) data and shipping terms are as seen in those extractions on 2026-10-10. The extractions are cached copies of unknown age, so some prices and stock labels may be stale. Many facts come only from the vendor's own pages. Where a fact comes from an affiliate blog, a Trustpilot review or an aggregator, the text says so. Where two pages disagree, both values are given.

## Bottom line

1. **The deciding criterion was public, per-batch purity information.** That means vendor-published per-lot third-party COAs with lot numbers, HPLC purity, MS identity and endotoxin or sterility results, plus independent public testing (Finnrick, TitrateLab) where it exists.
2. **No vendor meets the whole brief cleanly.** The brief asks for all five peptides as singles, one lot per peptide for the whole course, bacteriostatic water on the same site, fast shipping to Ohio, and good public batch data. Nobody was found who does all of that with independently confirmed data.
3. **Koi Peptides is the provisional choice** in the final ranking (transparency 5/10, fit 7/10). It sells bacteriostatic water on the same site and publishes a per-lot COA lookup. It is chosen with open gates, not with confidence. It has no standalone CJC-1295 no DAC (only a 5 mg/5 mg blend), a BPC-157 10 mg product page was not seen, and its only lab (Freedom Diagnostics) has a lab credibility score of 4/10. Four points are unconfirmed (see "Go/no-go gates for Koi").
4. **Panda Peptides is the best fallback that passes every filter except bacteriostatic water** (transparency 6, fit 7). It has true 5 mg CJC-1295 no DAC and Ipamorelin singles and the best lab mix (Janoshik and ILS). Water would come from a pharmacy.
5. **Protide Health has the newest per-lot COAs** (CJC-1295 no DAC lot PH-noda10-0630 dated 15 Sep 2026) but publishes no standalone bacteriostatic water listing, sells CJC-1295 and Ipamorelin in 10 mg only, and uses one lab (Freedom Diagnostics).
6. **Kylo Peptides** has the biggest-looking COA programme but the worst integrity problem: one lot ID (SD-260728-1) is reused on at least six different compounds. Transparency is scored 4.
7. **Verified Peptides** covers all five as singles with 2026 Janoshik-attributed COAs, but 18 of 36 Finnrick tests failed (BPC-157 44%, CJC-1295 31%, Ipamorelin 58%), and a reviewer alleges its Janoshik report numbers are fake. It sells no bacteriostatic water.
8. **Chameleon Peptides, the strongest vendor before closing, shut to new orders on 27 Sep 2026** and cannot be used. Peptide Sciences closed on 6 Mar 2026.
9. **Selling bacteriostatic water next to peptides is itself an enforcement risk.** FDA treats it as evidence of intent for human use. Every vendor that passes the "water on the same site" filter (Koi, Kylo, and in a free-vial form Protide) carries that exposure. No FDA letter to any of the five finalists was found, but absence from search results is not proof.
10. **No independent test data exist for the finalist vendors on the five target peptides**, except Verified Peptides (poor results) and a possibly unrelated "Super panda peptide" listing. Every positive COA claim below is a vendor claim that was not checked at the issuing lab.

## Method

### Task and filters

The research looked for a US research-peptide vendor that:

- sells GHK-Cu, TB-500, BPC-157, CJC-1295 (no DAC) and Ipamorelin;
- sells bacteriostatic water on the same site;
- ships quickly to Ohio;
- is operating on 2026-10-10;
- publishes per-batch purity data and, ideally, has independent public testing.

A stack-analysis panel had already decided the form of purchase: buy all five as separate single-peptide vials, with one lot per peptide for the whole course. A 1:1 CJC-1295/Ipamorelin 5 mg/5 mg blend is the only acceptable fallback, and only if the vendor lacks documented singles. GLOW, KLOW and BPC/TB blends are rejected. The judge added conditions: a lot-matched COA with HPLC purity, MS identity at the expected mass, net content and endotoxin, checkable on the lab's own portal. The panel advised sourcing USP bacteriostatic water from a pharmacy. The brief for this report still asks for water on the same site, so that filter was applied and its regulatory cost is noted.

### Stages

1. **Discovery.** Five search angles produced 17, 18, 15, 10 and 9 vendor leads (69 lead entries; many repeat across angles). Each angle also produced a blocklist.
2. **Profiling.** Thirteen vendors were profiled one by one (catalogue, prices, COA policy, labs, shipping, payment, reputation, FDA letters, red flags).
3. **Panel and judge.** The stack-analysis panel and judge set the purchase form and the vendor conditions above.
4. **Verification.** The five finalists (Protide Health, Kylo Peptides, Koi Peptides, Panda Peptides, Verified Peptides) each got an independent skeptic re-check of COA claims and a second live-status and catalogue pass. Each pass was limited to 12 searches.
5. **Lab credibility review.** Seven testing labs named by the vendors were assessed (14 searches).
6. **Ranking.** Transparency (0-10) and fit (0-10) scores were set, with adjustments from verification.

### Finnrick and TitrateLab explained

**Finnrick** (finnrick.com) buys or receives vial samples and sends them to labs for blind testing, then publishes results by vendor and by product.

- It publishes a percentage rating (a recency-weighted pass rate) and, on some product pages, an A to E grade.
- The default public standard, "Purity Focus", requires HPLC purity of at least 99.5% and fill within +/-35% of the label.
- The "Finnrick Standard" uses +/-25% and a label score of at least 0.5. The members-only "Super strict" standard uses 99.8%, +/-15% and a label score of at least 2.0.
- Thinly tested vendors are pulled toward the peer baseline, so a vendor with 2 tests gets a muted score.
- Finnrick accepts samples from the US only, and its directory has a Location column that is rarely filled in for US vendors.
- Some samples are vendor-submitted through Finnrick's "Launch with Finnrick" programme, which weakens their independence.
- Headline numbers often conflict between pages (for example Peptide Partners shows 103 tests on one page and 77 on another). Counts of vendors differ too (1,911 vendors in one directory view, 263 vendors and 8,687 tests in one guide).
- It does not cover bacteriostatic water.
- A Finnrick pass means the sampled vial met the standard on the test date. It says nothing about other lots.

**TitrateLab** (titratelab.com) aggregates COAs from third-party labs and grades vendors from those documents.

- Grades: A (certificate-backed), B (verified), C (tail-gated), D (thin evidence), F (failed). One failing batch floors the grade ("tail-gate").
- "In spec" means purity at or above 90% and dose within 10% of label.
- It gives a Trust Index out of 100 and a rank, and says scores come only from lab data with no vendor payment.
- Batch-level certificate detail is behind the paid TitrateLab Pro tier, so the researchers could see counts and averages but not individual certificates for most vendors.
- Its pages say they do not rank or recommend vendors, but its homepage shows a grade table. The full methodology and formula were not seen.
- Its Peptide Vendor Graveyard lists scams and closures (27 verified entries plus 13 unverified community claims on one page, 33 on the homepage, labelled a draft).
- Because TitrateLab mostly re-aggregates certificates a vendor or customer already holds, it is a check on documents more than an independent re-test.

**Other trackers seen but not relied on:** Watchtower Peptides (50+ vendors scored 0-100, commercial and self-published), PeptideCritic (directory and forum), PeptIQ, PeptideBenchmark, Peptigrity and the Janoshik public verification database (vendor opt-in). Many "best vendor" blogs were affiliate-driven and were treated as vendor claims.

## Transparency scoring rubric

Scores run 0 to 10. Bands as applied in the profiles:

| Band | Meaning | Examples in this report |
|---|---|---|
| 0-3 | No usable batch data, or no COAs at all | American Peptides (1) |
| 4-6 | COAs that are partial, representative, stale, gated or unverifiable, or that conflict with independent data | Koi (5), Kylo (4), Panda (6), Protide (6), Verified (6), Ascension (6), Core (4), BioLongevity (4), Polaris (4), Xcel (4), Tegridy (4, no vendor COA page found, third-party counts paywalled) |
| 7-9 | Public, lot-specific, third-party COAs from named labs with batch codes, covering the products, plus checkable lab IDs | Chameleon before closing (7). Protide, Panda and Verified were first scored 7 in their profiles, then cut to 6. Kylo and Koi were first scored 6. |
| 10 | Would need independent confirmation that agrees with the vendor data. Nobody reached it. | none |

Adjustments applied after verification: Protide 7 to 6, Kylo 6 to 4 (the profile's verification suggested 4; the ranking applied -2), Koi 6 to 5, Panda 7 to 6, Verified 7 to 6.

Points that raise a score: named lab, lot number, report ID or accession that can be looked up at the lab, recent date, MS identity, endotoxin and sterility values, lot printed on the vial, independent testing that agrees.

Points that lower it: reused or inconsistent lot IDs, conflicting purity figures, COAs older than the stock, "coming soon" notices, single-lab dependence, labs with no accreditation or no portal, unconfirmed lot-on-vial, independent testing that disagrees.

Fit score (0-10): coverage of the five peptides as singles, bacteriostatic water on site, price, stock, Ohio shipping speed and the regulatory profile.

Lab weighting used when reading COAs (from the lab review): Janoshik about 1.0 if the specific report resolves on its portal and matches the lot; Vanguard 0.9; ILS 0.8; Freedom Diagnostics 0.6; MZ Biolabs 0.5; Kovera 0.4; Optiq 0.2. A PDF that cannot be verified counts about 0.3 times the lab weight. These weights are the lab reviewer's judgement, not a published standard.

### Go/no-go gates for Koi

These four items were unconfirmed in the extractions. Confirm each before paying:

1. **Checkout is live today.** Newest dated Koi item seen was an Ipamorelin COA tested 20 Jun 2026. No closure notice appeared, but extractions cannot show a working checkout.
2. **Ohio is served.** The site ships to US addresses and no exclusion was seen. Ohio is not named. Carrier is not named.
3. **A BPC-157 10 mg single exists.** The catalogue lists "BPC-157 5 & 10mg" but no standalone product page, 10 mg price or stock was seen in two searches.
4. **The CJC/Ipamorelin blend COA is component-resolved.** The panel only accepts the 5/5 blend if the lot COA gives identity, quantity and purity for each component, ideally from a non-Janoshik lab. The Koi blend page says ">=99% HPLC per peptide", sterility "No Growth" and endotoxin "within limits", with no lot ID on the page.

Further checks the evidence supports (from the verification): ask which lot will ship for each peptide and whether one lot per peptide can cover the course; confirm the lot printed on the received vial matches a lookup entry; look up the lot's accession on Freedom Diagnostics' own site (the client name should read Koi); and treat support's reported statement that the vial QR code "doesn't confirm the batch/lot" as a warning.

Implications for reading COAs in this report:

1. No lab has a verified accreditation scope covering peptide HPLC purity or MS identity. A vendor claim of "ISO 17025 accredited testing" earns no credit unless a scope listing peptide methods is shown.
2. A vendor that uses Janoshik, Vanguard or ILS with report codes that resolve at the lab, for all five peptides, should outrank one using Optiq, Kovera or MZ. Kylo counts mainly through its Janoshik and Vanguard reports. Ascension (Kovera, MZ) is weakest on labs. Panda (Janoshik, ILS) scores well if its codes resolve. Protide and Koi (Freedom only) are moderate.
3. Endotoxin and sterility: only ILS (USP <85> endotoxin, a non-USP <71> sterility screen), Vanguard and some Janoshik batches show them. Missing data should be read as missing, not as a pass.
4. Fake or misattributed COAs are documented for Janoshik and Vanguard. Check each COA on the lab's own portal (task number, key, peptide, lot, date), not through a vendor link.
5. Even a genuine report certifies only the submitted sample, not the vial shipped.
6. Independent public testing, where it exists, should outweigh a vendor-commissioned COA.
