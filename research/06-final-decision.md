### Why these quantities

- **GHK-Cu, 3 vials.** The template dose of 1 mg/day for 12 weeks needs 84 mg. But vials are discarded 28 days after reconstitution, so it takes one vial per block: 3. Buy 4 if the GP sets 2 mg/day. Reconstitute 50 mg with 2 mL for 25 mg/mL, so 1 mg is 4 units.
- **BPC-157, 5 vials.** 500 mcg/day for 84 days is 42 mg, and a 10 mg vial lasts 20 days, so 5. If the GP fixes 250 mcg/day, buy 3 (one per 28-day block), saving \$119.90. 10 mg with 2 mL gives 5 mg/mL.
- **TB-500, 5 vials.** Six weeks of loading at 2.5 mg twice weekly (30 mg) plus 6 weeks at 2.5 mg weekly (15 mg) is 45 mg, so 5 vials. 10 mg with 2 mL gives 5 mg/mL, so 2.5 mg is 50 units. Drop this line if the GP judges TB-500 redundant with BPC-157.
- **Blend, 3 vials.** 100/100 mcg nightly is one vial per 28-day block, so 3. Buy 4 if dosing twice daily. 5/5 mg with 2.5 mL gives 2 mg/mL of each, so 100/100 mcg is 5 units. Koi sells no standalone CJC-1295 no DAC, so the blend is the only way to get CJC here. Koi does list ipamorelin as a single in 5 mg and 10 mg. The 5 mg page was not seen (likely \$69.95). The 10 mg is \$99.95, and its latest COA is accession 2606170096, 20 Jun 2026, 99.84%, net 10.13 mg. Buying that single would not solve the missing CJC single.
- **Water, 6 x 10 mL.** The blend replaces two singles, so the course needs 33.5 mL rather than 41 mL: GHK-Cu 3 x 2, BPC-157 5 x 2, TB-500 5 x 2, blend 3 x 2.5. The three 28-day blocks need about 12.5, 10.5 and 10.5 mL. Each block needs more than one 10 mL vial holds once vials are discarded 28 days after first puncture, so it takes two vials per block, 6 in all. A cheaper equivalent is 3 x 10 mL plus 3 x 3 mL (\$104.70, 39 mL, 13 mL per block), which saves \$15. Staggered starts spread reconstitutions beyond fixed blocks and may add about one vial, which is why 6 x 10 mL is kept as the default.

## 4. Separate vs stack verdict

Buy GHK-Cu, BPC-157 and TB-500 as separate single vials, one lot per peptide for the whole course. Use Koi's 1:1 5/5 mg CJC/Ipa blend only because Koi sells no standalone CJC-1295 no DAC, and only if the blend lot PDF shows MS identity, quantity and purity for each component plus an endotoxin value. If it does not, the rule is to change vendor. Here that means Panda's true 5 mg CJC-1295 no DAC and 5 mg ipamorelin singles with pharmacy water, not buying the blend unverified.

Rejected: GLOW, KLOW, the Wolverine BPC/TB blend and the GLOW Stack. Blends hide per-component identity and dose, and a failure in one component spoils the whole vial.

## 6. Legal and safety backdrop (as of 2026-10-10)

- None of the five is FDA-approved. FDA issued warning letters to research-peptide sellers on 31 Mar, 17 Jun and 24 Aug 2026 and treats bacteriostatic water sold alongside peptides as evidence of intent for human use.
- Peptide Sciences closed 6 Mar 2026. Chameleon Peptides closed to new orders 27 Sep 2026.
- There is no lawful injectable route for any of the five. The HHS stopgap reported on 7 Oct has not been issued.
- Non-injectable (topical) GHK-Cu is back in 503A Category 1, so an Ohio GP can prescribe it through a 503A pharmacy. Sermorelin (503A) or tesamorelin are named as alternatives to CJC/Ipa (not re-verified).
- FDA safety signals: ipamorelin remains in Category 2, with FDA citing immunogenicity and serious adverse events including a death after IV use. The CJC-1295 no DAC nomination was withdrawn, with FDA citing raised heart rate and a systemic vasodilatory reaction.

### 7.3 Verify and receive

- Look up each accession (for example 2604060224) yourself at freedomdiagnosticstesting.com. The client name must read Koi, not "Blank Peptides". A match proves only the name the sample was submitted under.
- On arrival, check that the lot printed on each vial matches its COA. Buyers have received QR-only vials, and Koi support reportedly said the QR code does not confirm the batch. If no lot is printed, raise it before using any vial.
- Save every PDF.
- Store unopened vials frozen and reconstituted vials at 2-8 C. Discard 28 days after first puncture.

### 7.4 GP oversight

- Baseline IGF-1, glucose/HbA1c, BP, serum copper and ceruloplasmin, plus age-appropriate cancer screening, with a 4-week review.
- Hard stops: cancer history, pregnancy or breastfeeding, Wilson's or another copper disorder, unmonitored diabetes or prediabetes, heart failure or oedema, pituitary disease, or drug-tested sport or military status.
- Stop rules: IGF-1 above range, rising glucose, oedema or carpal-tunnel symptoms, raised copper, injection-site infection, or brown or cloudy GHK-Cu.
- Stagger starts, adding one agent every 5-7 days.
- Have doses written in mcg and in syringe units, corrected for the measured net content on each COA.

### 7.5 Water and syringes

Pharmacy USP water is preferred. If the buyer accepts that, drop Koi's water (\$119.70). The same-site rule then no longer applies and Panda (transparency 6) outranks Koi. Koi sells no syringes. Buy U-100 0.3 mL 29-31G insulin syringes (about 3 boxes of 100), 3 mL reconstitution syringes, swabs and a sharps container from a Columbus pharmacy. A GP prescription removes any dispensing friction.

### 7.6 Payment and staging

## 11. How to verify a lot COA on arrival

Do this for every vial before use. Stop and contact the vendor if any step fails.

1. **Match the lot to the public report.** Read the lot printed on the vial label. Find the report with that lot on the vendor COA page and at the lab's own lookup (for Freedom, use the accession plus the first 4 characters of the client name). The client name must be the vendor's, and the lot or accession must match the vial. A QR code alone does not prove the batch. If the vial has no printed lot, do not use it until the vendor confirms the lot in writing.
2. **Check HPLC purity.** Look for the chromatogram, not just a percentage. One dominant peak, with purity at or above 98% (the vendor claims 99% or more). Check that the method and wavelength are stated. Treat a purity figure with no chromatogram as unverified.
3. **Check MS identity.** The report must give an observed mass that matches the expected mass: GHK about 340 Da (GHK-Cu about 402 Da), BPC-157 about 1,419 Da, TB-500 Ac-LKKTETQ about 889 Da, CJC-1295 no DAC about 3,368 Da (not about 3,647 Da, which is the DAC form), ipamorelin about 712 Da. The word "Confirmed" alone is not enough. For a blend, each component must be reported separately.
4. **Check net content.** Compare the tested quantity to the label. A large mismatch (under-fill, or large overfill such as 10.91 mg in a "10 mg" vial) means the dose maths is wrong. Correct syringe units for the measured net content.
5. **Check endotoxin and sterility.** Look for an endotoxin value in EU (EU/mg or EU/mL) and a sterility result. "Within limits" or "No Growth" with no number is a claim, not data. Missing endotoxin counts as a fail for injectable use.
6. **Compare the report date to the lot.** The report date should fall before or near the manufacture date of the lot, and not be many months old. The Koi TB-500 report is dated 30 Dec 2025, over 9 months before this order, so ask whether that lot still ships. A report older than the lot, or one dated after the vial was packed, is a red flag.
7. **Check the same lot is in every vial of a peptide.** One lot per peptide for the whole course. If vials show different lots, treat each lot as separate and ask for its report.
8. **Look at the vial.** The GHK-Cu cake should be blue. Do not use brown or cloudy GHK-Cu. Do not use any reconstituted solution that is cloudy or has particles. Discard 28 days after first puncture.
9. **Save everything.** Keep every PDF, the order email and photos of the labels. Consider keeping one unopened vial from each lot for independent HPLC plus MS testing.
